## Input provenance and exact frame

The sealed E12 text subset reproduces W1's 2,100 test items exactly: 84 items
per row, 16 structure rows and 9 semantic rows. E12's two Oracle phrasing pairs
per row exactly match W1, including their literal `- ` prefixes. Source hashes
are in INPUT_PROVENANCE.json. No wording was authored or selected.

The full string is `item.text + "\n" + candidate`, without a chat template,
question, adapter, injected state or special tokens. Tokenization is joint;
only tokens wholly within the candidate are scored. All 8,400 real-tokenizer
frames have an unambiguous boundary. Candidate scores are arithmetic means
of causal token log probabilities in nats, unlike W1's summed sequence scores.
The two candidate scores for a value are averaged per item before computing P
or D, matching W1's equal weighting of its two phrasings. P is then the median
true-candidate score across the 84 items. This aggregation is an implementation
choice made before E25 scores, not a change to a data-informed rule.

## Unresolved D definition — integrator ruling requested before launch

The registration says “AUROC of score(c_t) − score(c_w)” but does not identify
the binary target. Against the original A/B label, that truth-aligned margin
does not measure ordinary discrimination: equally good predictions of both
values can yield D = 0.5. The implementation preserves that literal A-positive
interpretation, and separately reports conventional AUROC of score(A) − score(B).
No silent substitution is made. The integrator must record a dated clarification
of the target before launch. If conventional D is chosen, publish a dated
amendment and matching analysis/plan revision before any E25 model scores.
The submitted plan has `integrator_acknowledged: false`; real analysis refuses
to join labels until this is resolved. Acquisition also requires the assignment's
dated D clarification. Changing an assignment alone does not bypass the analysis
plan acknowledgement or its score-seal binding.
