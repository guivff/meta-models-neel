## Limitation that frames everything (stated first on purpose)

The brief's sensitivity score is 1 − P(claim recurs under matched-norm random/control-vector readouts **of the same state**). Michael's release has **no per-state perturbed or control readouts**: `prior_consistency.jsonl` holds 20 GLOBAL Gaussian vectors at the mean activation norm (one shared set, not one per item), 1 mean vector and 40 real items, and for each only the Gemini-split claims of sample 1 with their support among samples 2–8 (146 random-vector claim strings in total; the readout texts are not released). `nla_samples.jsonl` carries only ['adjacent_readouts', 'family', 'id', 'samples'] per item. The 29 % figure is the share of random-vector claims repeated in ≥ 6 of 7 other samples (29 % recomputed here; real items 84 %).

**Proxy used instead** (fixed before any AUROC was computed): a claim's *prior recurrence* = the share of the 8 × 99 NLA readouts of the OTHER states of the same family that contain the claim; sensitivity = 1 − that share. A claim the NLA emits for other activations of the same family regardless of content is low-sensitivity. "Contains" is a lexical matcher: idf-weighted recall of the claim's stemmed content terms in the readout ≥ τ = 0.50. τ and the matcher were chosen **label-free** by agreement with his judged `sample_support_of_8` on the claim's own 8 samples (§2); they were never tuned on true/false labels. Secondary proxies: the same over all 499 other states (`sens_all`), and 1 − max recall against the 146 released random-vector claim strings (`sens_noise`). Caveats: (a) other-state readouts are not matched-norm random vectors — they carry other content of the same family, so this measures family-level genericity, not activation-independence; (b) the lexical matcher is a weak stand-in for his Gemini support judge (§2); (c) the item-level matched "control" of the brief would need S4-style per-state random-vector samples, which our pod stage S4 collects for our own readers, not for his NLA.

## 4. Reading

- **Why the pre-registered combination loses to its own inputs.** The LOFO coefficients are stable across the five folds (recurrence +2.01 to +3.22, sensitivity +2.36 to +4.68, same sign everywhere), but the fold intercepts run from -3.92 to -1.57 because the wrong-claim base rate differs by family (poetry 54 of 89, typo 1 of 167). Pooling out-of-fold decision scores across folds therefore mixes incompatible offsets, and the pooled AUROC of any *fitted* model is pushed down relative to a raw score, which has no fold offsets. Within each family (same fitted fold) "both" beats recurrence in the three families with more than one wrong claim and ties in the two with one (table below). This is a flaw of the pre-registered design, not evidence against the combination; the verdict above is nevertheless reported as pre-registered.

- **Post-hoc (dated 2026-10-11, after the primary run):** the parameter-free sum recurrence + sensitivity — the analogue of Michael's own "samples/8 + tokens/3" — scores 0.846 [0.804, 0.884] on internal claims, Δ vs recurrence +0.094 [+0.062, +0.125], and 0.828 [0.748, 0.893] on the ≥ 7/8 subset (Δ +0.273 [+0.191, +0.359]). It has no fitted parameters, so the caveat is not overfitting but that the choice of combination was made after seeing the LOFO failure (one post-hoc comparison). It would have met the pre-registered bar; it needs a fresh dataset (or the per-state control readouts of §5) to count. The in-sample logistic fit (reference only) gives the same numbers.
- **Sensitivity alone** already beats recurrence on internal claims (0.785 [0.732, 0.833] vs 0.752 [0.703, 0.799]; paired Δ +0.032 [-0.047, +0.103], CI includes 0). Mean sensitivity of wrong internal claims 0.72 vs correct 0.95; the gap is carried by poetry (0.47 vs 0.81: the repeated wrong claims there are generic "the next token must begin the second line" statements) and basic_readout; multihop and the one-wrong-claim families show none.
- **Input claims: the proxy runs backwards.** Sensitivity alone scores 0.27 (< 0.5): true background claims ("the text is prose", "a trivia question") are exactly the generic, family-wide statements the proxy calls insensitive, while misquotes are specific strings that recur nowhere else. The logistic fit learns the negative sign (input coefficient on sensitivity -0.86) and lands on recurrence alone; the unfitted sum, which assumes a positive sign, loses 0.05. So genericity helps only for claims about internal computation, where a generic claim is a confabulation risk; for claims about the input it marks truisms.

- The ≥ 7/8 wrong internal claims are the case the brief targets. Sensitivity alone orders them against the ≥ 7/8 correct claims at AUROC 0.83 [0.75, 0.89]; the adjacent-token check Michael already reports gives 0.67 there.
- Using the same lexical matcher on both sides (proxy recurrence + sensitivity vs proxy recurrence) gives Δ +0.099 [-0.010, +0.178] on internal claims: this is the apples-to-apples version, but proxy recurrence is itself a degraded copy of his judged recurrence (§2), so the primary comparison keeps his judged recurrence.
- On top of his best signal (recurrence + adjacent), sensitivity adds +0.066 [+0.012, +0.125] on internal claims.

## 5. What would settle it

Per-state control readouts: for each of the 500 items, 8 NLA samples on K random vectors of the item's norm (and ideally on the item's activation with its class-mean component removed), then his support judge on the same claims. Our pod stage S4 does this for our own readers (100 error states + 50 counts states, 50 random vectors); it does not cover his NLA or his claim set. Until then the sensitivity hypothesis ("consistent wrong claims are prior-generated and activation-invariant") is tested here only through family-level genericity.
