# Causal inference / statistics lens — ideation 2026-10-18

## What this lens sees that the current design (BRIEF_R/IDEAS_R/PLAN_R) does not
The DAG is: text T → state S_42 → (answer A; reader LO R reads S_42; probe P reads S_42). A reader LO is a valid
measurement of "the model's internal fact" only if R ⫫ T | S, i.e. once the state is fixed, nothing about the text
moves the reader. BRIEF_R tests this with one text-side reference (prompt-only, which is itself a child of S) and one
state-side reference (the probe). It has no *text-only* covariate, so "beyond the text" is never estimated as a
margin over a model-free text classifier; every exceedance can be explained by an unmeasured text feature
(length, last-token identity, the AI-vs-human final period, label frequency, template). Second, the decision rules
have never been powered: a +0.05 AUROC exceedance with CI > 0 at n = 200 has 27–43% power per cell at AUROC 0.8
(Hanley–McNeil, paired ρ 0.3–0.6), and 113 × 6 cells at α 0.05 yield ≈ 34 false exceedances under the null if the
rule is applied per cell without a family-wise step; 32 R5 items pass "≥ 75% with Wilson > 0.5" with 85% power at
true p 0.75 but the pass threshold is 22/32 = 69%; 48 R-C2 pairs split compliers/non-compliers at ≈ 24/24 and can
only detect a 40-point rate difference. Third, R5 facts are selected on probe(512) and prompt-only AUROC computed on
the same 200 test items whose 32 members are then used for estimation: a winner's curse on the fact level. Fourth,
the item bootstrap treats items as exchangeable, but items share templates and datasets share source corpora
(headlines 21–24, wikidata 56–64, borough/state/timezone 114–128), so fact-level CIs are too narrow. Fifth, R5
has random directions as its only placebo; it lacks negative-control exposures (a different fact's direction) and
negative-control outcomes (a different fact's reader on the same edited state), which are what separate
"reader follows this fact" from "reader follows any norm-matched offset". Every design below is a difference from
a control arm with a CI, reuses Monday's rows, and most run on CPU today.

## Designs

### CI-1 — The text ladder: bias-corrected reader score as partial AUROC over text-only classifiers   [cost: 0 pod-min, 3 CPU-h | slots into: WS3 / R2]
1. Claim: on SURFACE ∪ TOPIC ∪ FORM facts the median reader's AUROC conditional on a TF-IDF text-only score is ≤ 0.55, i.e. the reader carries no information about the label beyond what a bag-of-words model gets from the same text; on KNOWLEDGE facts the probe's conditional AUROC stays ≥ 0.75 and the readers' is ≥ 0.6.
2. Items/bank: R-A 113 test splits (200) + pools (512), both renders, every reader at its primary site; R-E KHOP/SYNTAX and R-X when collected.
3. Ground truths — TEXT: dataset label | STATE: probe(512) decision function at the same site, put through the same conditioning | MODEL: prompt-only LO put through the same conditioning (the model's own margin over text).
4. Controls: shuffled-state rows (conditional AUROC must be 0.5 ± 0.05), state-free prior, random-rotation J-lens (E13), and the ladder itself: nuisance-6 (token count, last-token id, digit/caps/punctuation rates, mean word length) ⊂ TF-IDF LR ⊂ prompt-only.
5. Decision rule: text-only models are fit on the pool with 5-fold CV and scored on test (leak-free). Partial AUROC = AUROC of the reader within each quintile of the text score, averaged, item bootstrap 1,000. BEYOND-TEXT(reader, fact) iff partial AUROC ≥ 0.60 with CI > 0.5. Per tag: fraction of BEYOND-TEXT cells with Wilson CI; readers compared with the probe by exact sign test on identical facts. Secondary: ANCOVA residual AUROC (reader LO regressed on the text score and the nuisance-6; AUROC of residuals) must agree with the partial AUROC within 0.05 on ≥ 90% of cells or the discrepancy is reported.
6. Cost: 0 pod-min; ≈ 113 × 2 renders × (TF-IDF LR + nuisance LR) ≈ 1,500 fits ≈ 1.5 CPU-h plus bootstraps ≈ 1.5 CPU-h. Reuses `analyze.reader_frame`, `auroc_ci`, `paired_auroc_diff`, `_cv_splits`, `load_bank`; new `text_ladder()` in `analyze.py`.
7. Outcomes: if SURFACE/TOPIC/FORM partial AUROC ≤ 0.55 and KNOWLEDGE ≥ 0.6 → the H2 split holds against a text-only classifier, which is a stronger statement than against the prompt-only ceiling (the ceiling is a child of the state). If readers stay BEYOND-TEXT on SURFACE/TOPIC while the probe does too → the TF-IDF model is too weak; escalate the ladder to an LLM text classifier (E10) before claiming anything. If KNOWLEDGE partial AUROC ≈ 0.5 for readers but ≥ 0.75 for the probe → readers do not read retrieved content; H2's positive clause fails with a text covariate in place. Null (everything at 0.5, probe included) → the fact is text-determined; drop it from the beyond-text map.
8. Novelty: Nanda's review suggested TF-IDF twins for J-lens detectors; nobody has used a text-only score as a conditioning covariate for a verbalizer, or reported reader value as a partial AUROC. Differs from E10 (inversion baseline uses the reader's own free text) and from R2 (prompt-only is model-side). Adds a column to F1/F3.

### CI-2 — Nuisance-stratified AUROC and the final-period confounder   [cost: 0 pod-min, 1 CPU-h | slots into: WS3 / R2, R4]
1. Claim: for ≥ 20% of (fact, reader) cells at the raw render the reader's AUROC drops by ≥ 0.10 when computed within strata of last-token identity and token-count tercile, i.e. a measurable share of reader AUROC at the raw site is carried by nuisance features of the read position, and the chat render (fixed assistant-onset token) removes it.
2. Items/bank: R-A test splits, both renders; AI-vs-human 94/110 (the paper's final-period cue), CoLA 87, clickbait 105 as pre-named cases.
3. Ground truths — TEXT: label | STATE: probe(512) stratified the same way (a probe can exploit the cue; its stratified drop is the "cue is in the state" reference) | MODEL: prompt-only LO stratified the same way.
4. Controls: shuffled rows (stratified AUROC 0.5), state-free, random-rotation J-lens; and the label–nuisance association itself (Cramér's V between label and last-token id, |ρ| between label and length) reported per fact so a drop is only claimed where the nuisance is confounded with the label (V ≥ 0.2 or |ρ| ≥ 0.2).
5. Decision rule: ΔAUROC_strat = pooled AUROC − stratum-weighted AUROC (Mantel–Haenszel style, strata with ≥ 10 items per class), item bootstrap CI. NUISANCE-CARRIED(cell) iff ΔAUROC_strat ≥ 0.10 with CI > 0.05. The claim holds iff the Wilson CI of the cell fraction at raw excludes 0.20 from below and the chat-render fraction is lower by a paired sign test over facts (p < 0.05).
6. Cost: 0 pod-min, ≈ 1 CPU-h (stratified AUROCs + 1,000 bootstraps on 113 × 6 × 2 cells). Reuses `cells_table`, `auroc_ci`, the tokenizer for last-token ids from `states/<ds>/index.json`.
7. Outcomes: if the raw-render drop is concentrated in cells where the probe also drops → the cue is in the state and both instruments exploit it (a dataset artefact, reported as in the paper's 4.3.2); if readers drop and the probe does not → readers read token identity at the read position, a text-inversion mechanism with a named feature; if the chat render shows the same drops → the cue survives the assistant onset and R4's render-shift result must be qualified. Null (no cell ≥ 0.10) → nuisance confounding is negligible on this bank; stated as a cleared threat.
8. Novelty: the final-period cue is known for probes (SAE-probing 4.3.2) but no reader has been scored within nuisance strata; this gives the "read position carries the label" diagnosis a number rather than a case study (C2).

### CI-3 — Difference-in-differences on the R-C2 context pairs with non-compliers as the negative-control exposure   [cost: 0 pod-min beyond WS5, 0.5 CPU-h | slots into: WS5 / R3]
1. Claim: the reader's context effect (LO toward the context answer, false-context minus true-context turn, identical visible prompt) is the same on compliers and non-compliers within 0.5 nats, i.e. readers at 42–48 report the context, not the belief that drives the model's answer; by 56 the complier minus non-complier difference is ≥ 1 nat for the Oracle and J-lens.
2. Items/bank: R-C2 48 pairs (false fact in the system turn vs the true fact in the same turn, both candidate orders), V7c 48 private-fact pairs as the "no conflict" reference; E7's paraphrases if collected.
3. Ground truths — TEXT: the context's answer | STATE: probe trained on pool pairs for "context answer present" | MODEL: prompt-only greedy choice (complier vs non-complier) and the model's LO.
4. Controls: shuffled state, state-free prior, candidate-order counterbalance (both roles), the true-context arm (the reader must not move when the context agrees with the belief), and the non-complier arm as negative-control exposure (the context is in the text but does not change behaviour).
5. Decision rule: DiD = E[ΔLO | complier] − E[ΔLO | non-complier], pair bootstrap 5,000. READS-CONTEXT(reader, layer) iff |DiD| < 0.5 nats and the CI covers 0 while E[ΔLO | all] ≥ 1 nat with CI > 0; READS-BELIEF iff DiD ≥ 1 nat with CI > 0. Power note: with ≈ 24/24 split the rate-scale MDE is 40 points; the LO-scale DiD has MDE ≈ 2.8 × sd/√24 ≈ 0.6 nat at sd 1, so verdicts are on LO only; if compliers are < 12 or > 36 of 48, the DiD is reported as underpowered and only the pooled context effect is claimed.
6. Cost: 0 pod-min beyond WS5's collection; 0.5 CPU-h. Reuses `collect_reader_lo` rows with `system` set, `r3_conflict`, `common.stats.bootstrap_ci`.
7. Outcomes: READS-CONTEXT at 42–48 and READS-BELIEF at 56 → H3's lag law confirmed on a design where text and behaviour are decoupled by the model's own choice. READS-BELIEF already at 42 → readers read the model earlier than Y1/V1 suggested; the lag was a puzzle-family property. READS-CONTEXT at every layer → readers never read the belief; a monitor built on them reports what was said to the model. Null (no context effect at all) → the V7 success does not survive conflict; V7 was text inversion of a consistent system turn.
8. Novelty: R-C2 as written compares compliers vs non-compliers descriptively; the DiD with the true-context arm and a pre-powered LO-scale rule is new, and it is the first use of the model's own non-compliance as a negative-control exposure for a reader.

### CI-4 — Placebo and negative-control edits for R5: other-fact directions, post-read-site edits, other-fact outcomes   [cost: 30 pod-min, 1 CPU-h | slots into: WS4 / R5]
1. Claim: a reader's follow under d_cm(f) is fact-specific: it exceeds (a) its follow under the norm-matched d_cm(g) of a different fact g by ≥ 0.5 of its natural separation and (b) the follow of fact g's candidates read on the same d_cm(f)-edited states by ≥ 0.5; and an edit at block 44 produces zero change in the block-42 read-back (pipeline placebo).
2. Items/bank: the 30 R5 facts × 32 items; g = the R5 fact of a different tag with the nearest class-mean norm (pre-registered pairing table written at `--r5-select` time).
3. Ground truths — TEXT: unchanged (the text is fixed under every edit) | STATE: probe(512) score on the edited read-back state, for f and for g | MODEL: the model's answer LO on f's question and on g's question under the same edit.
4. Controls: K = 10 random directions (existing band), d_cm(g) as a structured negative-control exposure, g's reader as a negative-control outcome, the post-read-site edit (edit 44 → read 42; any movement is a leak), zero-delta identity (WS4 pre-check), shuffled-state rows.
5. Decision rule: specificity ratio = follow_f(d_cm(f)) / max(follow_f(d_cm(g)), follow_g(d_cm(f))), per fact, pair bootstrap over items. SPECIFIC(reader, f) iff follow_f(d_cm(f)) − max(...) ≥ 0.5 of natural separation with CI > 0. The claim holds iff SPECIFIC on ≥ 75% of facts (Wilson CI > 0.5). Pipeline placebo: post-site read-back |Δ| < 1e-3 on every item, else WS4 stops. Non-specific cells are removed from the REPORT-FLIPPABLE / READER-BLIND tallies and counted separately.
6. Cost: two extra directions per fact at α = 1 only (30 × 32 × 2 × 2 forwards ≈ 3,840 ≈ 20 pod-min) plus the block-44 placebo on 5 facts (≈ 5 pod-min) and g-candidate read-backs (J-lens free, Oracle ≈ 2k scores ≈ 1 pod-min). Reuses `edited_forward_batch` (with `layer=44`), `read_back`, `directions_for`, `save_directions`; analysis in `r5_analyze`.
7. Outcomes: SPECIFIC everywhere → R5 verdicts stand as fact-level claims. Non-specific on many facts (a reader follows any class-mean-sized offset of the same norm) → "report-flippable" is a property of the reader's sensitivity to norm-matched offsets, not of fact geometry; the safety implication is stronger (any added direction moves the report) but H5's cos claims need re-reading. Placebo leak → stop and fix before any R5 number is quoted. Null (follows nothing, including d_cm(f)) → the fact is not readable at 42 under edits; reported as such.
8. Novelty: R5 and E5 use random directions and cross-reader transfer; neither has a structured other-fact direction or an other-fact outcome. This is the standard negative-control pair from epidemiology (Lipsitch et al.) applied to activation edits for the first time.

### CI-5 — Power audit, hierarchical multiple-comparison control and the surprise-replication rule, fixed before Monday's data   [cost: 0 pod-min now, ≤ 10 pod-min for replications, 2 CPU-h | slots into: BRIEF_R §4 and §9 (pre-freeze), WS3]
1. Claim: under the current per-cell rules the expected number of false R2 exceedances across 113 × 6 primary-site cells is ≥ 10 and the +0.05 rule has < 45% power at AUROC 0.8, n = 200; a BH step at the fact level (q = 0.10, per reader) with a fresh-item replication for every flagged cell keeps false exceedances ≤ 1 while losing ≤ 20% of true +0.10 effects.
2. Items/bank: Monday's shuffled rows (the empirical null: label-permuted AUROCs per cell) and the mock (`r_bench/mock.py`) for the alternative; replication items = pool items 65–264 of a flagged fact, never used for selection.
3. Ground truths — TEXT: label permutation null | STATE: probe(512) under the same null (its false-positive rate is the calibration check) | MODEL: prompt-only under the same null.
4. Controls: the shuffled rows are the control arm by construction; the mock's known effects give power.
5. Decision rule (proposed for BRIEF_R §4 before the freeze; a dated Deviations note if adopted after): (i) all CIs stay; (ii) inferential claims are made at the primary site and render only, the 11-layer × 2-render sweep is descriptive; (iii) per reader, the 113 fact-level p-values for each rule (R2 exceedance, R3 verdict, R4 lag) go through BH at q = 0.10; (iv) any cell that passes and is "surprising" (pre-listed: an exceedance on SURFACE/TOPIC/FORM, a READS-MODEL verdict at 42, a non-reader-blind cell in R5) is re-scored on 200 fresh pool items; it is claimed only if the replication passes the same rule one-sided at α 0.05; (v) the MDE table below is printed in `summary.md` next to every rule. MDE at 80% power, one-sided 2.5%: paired AUROC diff 0.08–0.10 (AUROC 0.8, n = 200), 0.11–0.15 (n = 100); 32-item rate rule passes at 22/32, power 0.85 at true p 0.75; fact-level mean ΔAUC 0.008–0.021 over 113 facts (sd 0.03–0.08); sign test over 113 facts detects P(win) 0.65 with power 0.88 but 0.60 with only 0.53; two-arm rate difference with 24/24 pairs MDE 0.40.
6. Cost: 0 pod-min for the audit (≈ 2 CPU-h: permutation AUROCs from the `|shuf` rows, BH, mock power curves); replications ≈ 200 items × ≤ 20 flagged cells × Oracle ≈ 4k scores ≈ 1 pod-min per reader, J-lens free, NLA ≤ 10 pod-min. Reuses `shuffled_check`, `r2_ceiling`, `mock.py`, `auroc_ci`.
7. Outcomes: if the empirical null from the shuffled rows is wider than the bootstrap CIs assume (e.g. 5% of shuffled cells outside [0.45, 0.55] but at n = 100 the Wilson band is ±0.10) → the pipeline-bug threshold in §4.2 is miscalibrated for short datasets and must be n-dependent. If BH removes most exceedances → the raw map over-claims; report both. Null (per-cell rules already calibrated, ≤ 1 expected false exceedance) → the audit is a one-line footnote.
8. Novelty: BRIEF_R §9 lists "multiple comparisons: pre-stated rules; CIs everywhere" with no family-wise procedure and no power statement; no reader-evaluation paper reports MDEs. This design changes nothing after data; it writes the rules Monday morning.

### CI-6 — E-value sensitivity: how strong must an unmeasured text feature be to explain a reader's beyond-text margin?   [cost: 0 pod-min, 1 CPU-h | slots into: WS3 / R2, WS8 trust rule]
1. Claim: on KNOWLEDGE and CONTEXT facts where a reader is BEYOND-TEXT (CI-1), the correlation r* that a single unmeasured text feature would need with both the label and the reader LO to drive the partial AUROC to 0.5 exceeds the strongest measured text feature's correlation by ≥ 0.2; on SURFACE/TOPIC facts r* is within 0.1 of a measured feature.
2. Items/bank: the BEYOND-TEXT cells from CI-1 (R-A, R-E, R-C2, V7c).
3. Ground truths — TEXT: the measured text-feature correlations (TF-IDF LR score, nuisance-6) as the benchmark for r* | STATE: the probe's own r* (an upper bound for what a state-based instrument can show) | MODEL: prompt-only's r*.
4. Controls: shuffled rows (r* undefined/0 by construction), state-free; the probe as the reference instrument.
5. Decision rule: convert the partial AUROC to a standardised mean difference d = √2 Φ⁻¹(AUROC) and then to a risk ratio on the median split (VanderWeele & Ding); E = RR + √(RR(RR − 1)). Report E per cell with the item-bootstrap CI and the "measured E" of the TF-IDF score; ROBUST(cell) iff E_reader − E_measured ≥ 1.0 (equivalently r* ≥ r_measured + 0.2 on the correlation scale, checked by simulation: add a synthetic feature with correlation r to label and reader, find r where partial AUROC reaches 0.5). The claim holds iff ROBUST on ≥ 75% of KNOWLEDGE ∪ CONTEXT BEYOND-TEXT cells (Wilson CI > 0.5).
6. Cost: 0 pod-min, ≈ 1 CPU-h (closed-form E-values plus a 20-point r grid × 200 bootstraps per cell). New `evalue()` in `common/stats.py`; reuses CI-1's partial AUROC frame.
7. Outcomes: ROBUST on KNOWLEDGE/CONTEXT → the beyond-text claim survives any text feature as strong as the ones we can measure; this is the sentence the trust rule needs. Not ROBUST (E_reader ≈ E_measured) → the reader's margin is explainable by one more text feature of ordinary strength; the beyond-text claim is downgraded to "not yet excluded". Null (no BEYOND-TEXT cells) → nothing to sensitise.
8. Novelty: E-values have not been applied to interpretability readouts; they turn "we controlled for text" into "a text confounder would need strength X", which is what a reviewer asks.

### CI-7 — Leak-free selection with split-sample estimation and the winner's-curse estimate for R5 and R-C1   [cost: 0 pod-min, 1 CPU-h | slots into: WS3 `--r5-select`, WS4]
1. Claim: selecting the 30 R5 facts on test-split probe(512) and prompt-only AUROC, then estimating on 32 of the same test items, inflates the selected facts' mean probe AUROC by ≥ 0.02 and changes ≥ 5 of the 30 facts relative to selection on the pool_eval items (64 pool items scored by every reader at the primary site, probe via inner CV on the pool).
2. Items/bank: R-A; the pool_eval rows in `reader_lo.jsonl` (condition "natural", split "pool"), probe fits on the pool with `_cv_splits`.
3. Ground truths — TEXT: label | STATE: probe AUROC on pool_eval (selection) vs on test (estimation) | MODEL: prompt-only AUROC on pool (the prompt-only pass covers all 712 items) vs test.
4. Controls: a split-half of the test set (100/100) as a second selection route; the shuffled rows (selection on noise must pick facts at random).
5. Decision rule: curse = mean_selected(AUROC_test) − mean_selected(AUROC_selection-sample), fact bootstrap 1,000; the claim holds iff the CI of the curse under test-selection excludes 0 and the pool-selected list differs by ≥ 5 facts. Rule for BRIEF_R (pre-freeze): R5 selection uses pool_eval and pool-CV probe numbers only; the 32 estimation items are test items; R-C1 conflict items are selected on the model's greedy pick (a variable the readers are not scored on), so that selection is leak-free as written and is left unchanged.
6. Cost: 0 pod-min, ≈ 1 CPU-h (probe CV fits on 64–512 pool items for 113 facts). Reuses `r5_select` (add `--select-on pool`), `probe_fit_select`, `balanced_draw`.
7. Outcomes: curse ≥ 0.02 → R5's fact list is re-selected before Wednesday's pod session and the deviation is dated; curse ≈ 0 → the test-selected list stands and the analysis is cited as evidence it is unbiased. Null (fewer than 30 facts pass on pool_eval because n = 64 is noisy) → relax to probe(512)-CV ≥ 0.9 on the full pool with prompt-only ≥ 0.9 on pool, documented.
8. Novelty: the quiver is leak-free by design (R1) but the R5 and C-case selections are not split-sample; the winner's-curse magnitude for interpretability fact selection has never been reported.

### CI-8 — Heterogeneous effects: an honest tree over fact features for the reader minus probe gap   [cost: 0 pod-min, 2 CPU-h | slots into: WS3 / F3, WS8 trust rule]
1. Claim: three pre-registered moderators (CI-1's text-ladder AUROC, cos(d_cm, d_probe) at 42, the state-free prior's |LO| for the fact's candidates) explain ≥ 50% of the held-out variance of the reader minus probe(512) AUROC gap across facts, and tag alone explains < 30%.
2. Items/bank: all cells of `cells.csv` (R-A, R-B pairs, R-D, R-E, R-X, R-B) at the primary site and render; one row per (fact, reader).
3. Ground truths — TEXT: text-ladder AUROC (how text-determined the fact is) | STATE: probe(512) AUROC and cos(d_cm, d_probe) from the pool states | MODEL: prompt-only AUROC and the model's greedy error rate.
4. Controls: a permutation null for R² (fact labels shuffled 200×), the shuffled-state rows (gap defined against them must be unexplainable), and the tag-only model as the baseline.
5. Decision rule: honest estimation: facts split 50/50 (seeded); a depth-≤ 3 regression tree and a gradient-boosted model fit on half A, R² and partial dependence estimated on half B, fact bootstrap 1,000 for the R² CI. Claim holds iff R²_B(3 moderators) ≥ 0.5 with CI > 0.3 and R²_B(tag) < 0.3. Exploratory (labelled): other features (J-lens single-token coverage, candidate token frequency, token-count mean, label–length |ρ|, dataset size, sidedness) with their partial dependence.
6. Cost: 0 pod-min, ≈ 2 CPU-h including CI-1 inputs. Reuses `cells_table`, `r5_select` directions (d_cm, d_probe at 42 are computed for every fact at `--r5-select` time; extend to all 113 facts: ≈ 113 probe fits).
7. Outcomes: ≥ 0.5 explained → the trust rule becomes a fitted model: "reader AUROC ≈ probe AUROC when the fact is text-light, cos(d_cm, d_probe) ≥ x and the reader's prior is flat", with the partial dependence plots as F3's companion. Tag explains more than the moderators → tags encode something the three measurements miss; report what. Null (nothing explains ≥ 0.3) → reader failure is idiosyncratic per fact; no compact trust rule exists, which is itself the result.
8. Novelty: no reader study models failure as a function of fact-level covariates; the SAE-probing paper diagnosed failures case by case. The honest split is the causal-forest discipline applied to a 113-fact table.

### CI-9 — Interference: cluster-robust CIs over source corpora and template-held-out replication   [cost: 0 pod-min, 1.5 CPU-h | slots into: WS3 (all regimes), WS12 ReaderBench]
1. Claim: a two-stage bootstrap (source corpus → dataset → item) widens the fact-level CIs of the headline means (quiver ΔAUC(n), exceedance count, consensus gain) by ≥ 1.3× relative to the i.i.d. fact bootstrap; and on ReaderBench facts a reader's AUROC changes by < 0.05 under template hold-out while probe(n ≤ 16) drops by ≥ 0.10.
2. Items/bank: R-A with a pre-written corpus map (headlines 21–24, wikidata 56–64, borough/state/timezone/country 114–128, bigram/phrase 65–85, code 158–163, GLUE 87–91); R-B (WS12) facts whose items carry a `template` id.
3. Ground truths — TEXT: label | STATE: probe(n) refit on the template-held-in items only | MODEL: prompt-only on the held-out template.
4. Controls: the i.i.d. fact bootstrap (existing), shuffled rows (cluster bootstrap must still centre at 0.5), the random-rotation J-lens.
5. Decision rule: CI width ratio = cluster-bootstrap width / i.i.d. width per headline statistic, 1,000 resamples each; the claim holds iff the ratio ≥ 1.3 for at least two of three headline statistics. Template shift: for each ReaderBench fact with ≥ 4 templates, leave one template out; Δ_reader = AUROC_held-out − AUROC_held-in (readers are zero-shot, so held-in is the same score on different items); Δ_probe(n) likewise with the probe trained on held-in items; paired sign test over facts at n = 8 and 16. RENDER-OF-TEMPLATE-ROBUST(method) iff |Δ| ≤ 0.05 on ≥ 80% of facts.
6. Cost: 0 pod-min, ≈ 1.5 CPU-h (bootstraps + ≈ 60 facts × 4 templates × 2 n × 5 seeds probe fits ≈ 2,400 fits). Reuses `quiver_summary`, `mean_ci`, `_r1_one` with a template mask.
7. Outcomes: ratio ≥ 1.3 → every fact-level CI in `summary.md` is reported from the cluster bootstrap and some H1/H7 verdicts may lose significance; this is reported as the honest width. Readers template-robust, probes not → the one regime where a zero-shot reader beats a small probe is covariate shift, a practitioner rule the quiver at fixed n cannot show. Null (ratio ≈ 1, both robust) → items are exchangeable enough; stated once.
8. Novelty: the SAE-probing paper has a covariate-shift regime for probes but no clustered inference; no reader study has reported either. Extends R1/R4 with a shift arm and fixes the CI machinery.

### CI-10 — Transplanted-label negative-control outcome: does the reader's fact-f score separate an unrelated fact g?   [cost: 4 pod-min, 0.5 CPU-h | slots into: WS2 (session 2) / R2, R7]
1. Claim: for ≥ 20% of (reader, fact) cells the reader's score with fact f's candidates, computed on the states of an unrelated fact g of a different tag, separates g's labels with AUROC ≥ 0.65, i.e. the score is a generic text-property axis (sentiment, formality, length) rather than a fact-specific reading; the prompt-only model asked f's question on g's text does so in fewer cells.
2. Items/bank: 20 pre-registered (f, g) pairs across tags from R-A (e.g. sentiment 92 on headlines 21, toxic on CoLA 87, spam on AI-vs-human 94, timezone 120 on sciq), 200 test items of g each.
3. Ground truths — TEXT: g's label | STATE: the f-probe (trained on f's pool) applied to g's states, AUROC against g's label (does the state itself carry the cross-fact correlation?) | MODEL: prompt-only with f's question on g's text, AUROC against g's label.
4. Controls: shuffled rows (0.5), state-free prior, the f-reader on f's own states (positive control), the random-rotation J-lens; |AUROC − 0.5| is the statistic so either sign counts.
5. Decision rule: CROSS-FACT(reader, f, g) iff |AUROC − 0.5| ≥ 0.15 with the item-bootstrap CI excluding 0.5. The claim holds iff the Wilson CI of the cell fraction excludes 0.20 from below and the reader fraction exceeds the prompt-only fraction by a paired sign test over pairs (p < 0.05). A cell where the f-probe is also CROSS-FACT is labelled "state-borne" and excluded from the reader-specific count.
6. Cost: Oracle 20 pairs × 200 × 2 renders ≈ 8k scores ≈ 2 pod-min; skip-lens ≈ 1 pod-min; NLA ≈ 1 pod-min in phase B; J-lens free; prompt-only cross questions 4k forwards ≈ 1 pod-min. Reuses `collect_reader_lo` with a `--candidates-from f --states-from g` option (small change), `collect_prompt_only` with the swapped question; analysis via `auroc_ci`.
7. Outcomes: reader cross-fact fraction ≥ 0.2 and above prompt-only → reader scores are not fact-specific instruments; the R7 "readers behave as one instrument" result gets a mechanism (they share a generic axis), and every AUROC in F3 needs the transplanted-label twin next to it. Reader fraction ≈ prompt-only fraction → the cross-fact signal is a real text correlation the model also has; readers are no worse than the model. Null (fraction < 0.05 for every method) → reader scores are fact-specific; a cleared threat and a stronger F3.
8. Novelty: the transplanted-label twin appears in Nanda's J-lens review as a suggestion for free-text detectors; nobody has run it on native-LO readers against a probe and prompt-only reference. Not in E1–E15; it is the negative-control outcome R2 lacks.
