## Context

Phases X–Z showed that at a fixed site the readers (J-lens, Oracle Lens, NLA, skip-lens A) report what is linearly present and follow edits the model ignores (J-space component, template tokens: 0–11% model flips vs 78–95% reader follow). A probe reads the model's wrong puzzle answer at layer 48 (79%) where readers are at 41–57%, but that probe has no causal check. A parallel project (Michael Kenney, `nla-consistency`) found that the NLA follows class-mean directions of an operand but ignores the probe-weight directions, and that the model also ignores those probe-weight directions. So part of a probe's lead can come from directions nobody uses.

## Hypothesis (pre-register this verbatim)

**H-W: Readers are class-mean readers.** Each reader follows the class-mean direction of a fact (the shift in the mean activation when the fact changes) whether or not the model uses it, and does not follow the residual directions a probe relies on. The model's answer depends on more than the class-mean direction for at least one family.

Predictions: (P1) editing the class-mean component alone moves every reader ≥ 75% of states; (P2) editing the probe-weight residual alone moves readers ≤ 60% (inside the random band) while moving the probe ≥ 80%; (P3) for at least one family, the model's flip rate under class-mean-only editing is at least 20 points below the full-swap flip rate. Reader difference threshold as in BRIEF_Z (ΔAUROC ≥ 0.05 or Δwin ≥ 10 points with CI excluding 0).

Write the pre-registration (W hypothesis and thresholds, V thresholds) into `projects/g_agreement/PREREG_WV.md` and commit it **before** any analysis or pod start.

## Readers

## Tracks that run in parallel on CPU (before the pod)

Launch A, B and C as three subagents at the same time.

### Track A: graded bank (new prompts, not Michael's)

Generate under `projects/g_agreement/banks/w/`, seeds saved, with the same template machinery as Z5:

| Family | Slot | Values | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| counts | "There are N <object> in the <place>." … "How many <object>?" | N = 1–99 | 600 prompts (6 per value, 4 templates, 3 objects) |
| mapping-8 | end symbol of the arrow chain (existing bank) | 8 symbols | reuse the 47 mapping pairs; score all 8 candidates |
| ownership-4 | which of 4 names holds the object | 4 names, Z5 factorial design | 128 prompts |
| arithmetic bridge | "a ± b =" | a, b ∈ 0–99 | 100 prompts, generated by us with Michael's format (for comparability only) |

Checks before the pod: for each family, regress PC1 of within-family activation differences on the design factors; no single factor may explain > 25% of variance (as in Z5). For counts, confirm the model answers correctly ≥ 95% (greedy); keep only values it gets right.

### Track B: direction decomposition on cached states

On the cached layer-38 states (ownership, mapping; X2/Z4 bank) and layer-48 last-token states (297 error puzzles; Y1 cache), compute per family:

1. **Class-mean direction** d_cm: difference of class means (for errors: mean over states where the actual answer is symbol s, minus mean over the rest, per s; use the pair's own two candidates).
2. **Probe-weight direction** d_pw: the held-out logistic probe's weight vector (inner CV as in Y), orthogonalised against d_cm.
3. **J-space component** d_J: projection of the swap vector onto the top-384 J-space (existing basis).
4. **Remainder** d_res: swap vector minus (d_cm + d_pw + d_J components), orthogonalised.

Also fit, per family, Michael's recipe exactly: 18 class-mean directions and 18 probe-weight directions for the graded slot (counts, arithmetic), so the bridge is comparable.

Write all steering vectors, per pair and per family, to `cache/g_agreement/w/directions/` with norms recorded. Report the fraction of the full swap's norm in each component.

### Track C: claim-level scoring (API only)

Adapt `nla-consistency/scripts/claim_judge.py` (Michael's; cite it) to our existing Oracle and NLA free-text readouts on the X1 binding states and the X3 error states. Label each claim true/false against the prompt and, where applicable, against the model's actual answer. Output per-reader claim precision with item-level bootstrap CI. Use one judge; flag any claim about internal computation for a second judge pass on a 100-claim subsample and report κ.

## The pod session (one tmux session, checkpointed stages)

Order the stages so the most important results exist first if the budget runs out.

**S1: Capture the new bank** (all of Track A) **plus the V7 target-private pairs** (16 pairs, see Part V). Hook layers 20, 28, 38, 42, 48, 52 and all positions in one forward pass per prompt. Record the model's greedy answer and first-token log-odds. Checkpoint.

**S2: Steering matrix at layer 38 (ownership, mapping) and layer 48 (errors).** For each family and each direction family {d_cm, d_pw, d_J, d_res, full swap, random (K = 20, matched norm)}, edit at matched norm at the edit sites from Z4 (ownership: the span after the statements; mapping: the two differing symbols; errors: last token), dose α ∈ {0.5, 1.0}. For every edited state record: model answer, Δ answer log-odds, read-back block-42 (and block-48 for errors) last-token vector. Target ≈ 4,000 decodes. Checkpoint after each family.

**S3: Reader scoring, plus the V6 multi-position Oracle arm** (~15 min, see Part V). Score J-lens, Oracle, skip-lens A and the probe on every S1 and S2 read-back vector. Then the NLA, on the S2 states and the counts family only (NLA is the slow reader). Checkpoint after each reader.

**S4: Consistency null.** On the 297 error states and 100 counts states: 8 NLA samples and 8 Oracle samples per state at the native temperature; plus 50 random vectors of matched norm. Checkpoint.

**S5: Stop the pod.** Write STATUS.md, rsync results, then stop.

## Analysis (CPU, four subagents in parallel after S5)

**W1 matrix (primary).** Rows: direction family × task family. Columns: model flip rate (Wilson CI), and for each reader the follow rate (score moves toward the edit; pair-level bootstrap CI) and the Z1-style ΔAUROC over dose-only. Evaluate P1–P3. Figure: `w/fig_matrix.png`, one heat map of follow rate with model flip rate as a separate column.

**W2 precision on graded facts.** For counts, arithmetic and mapping-8: per reader, exact-match rate, Spearman between reported and true value, and a confusion structure (|error| histogram). Native log-odds over all candidate values; for Oracle and NLA also the number they state in free text. Report "keeps magnitude, drops digits" per reader as (Spearman, exact-match). Figure: `w/fig_precision.png`.

**W3 claims.** Per-reader claim precision on input vs computation claims; cross-tab with native log-odds correctness on the same items.

**W4 consistency.** Does keeping claims in ≥ 2 of 8 samples raise favours-actual on errors or exact-match on counts? Report AUROC of agreement count vs correctness, and the random-vector self-consistency rate. Pre-registered expectation (from Michael): no gain on internal computation.

**Bridge.** On the 100 arithmetic prompts report the same numbers Michael reports (probe exact-match for a and b, NLA exact-match, Spearman). State whether they lie within his CIs.

## Decision rules

- P1, P2 and P3 all hold → headline: "readers read class-mean directions; the model uses more". Rewrite the layer-48 claim as "the model's wrong answer is load-bearing along d_cm at layer 48 and the readers still miss it on natural states" **only if** d_cm steering at 48 flips the model ≥ 50%.
- d_cm steering at 48 does not flip the model and d_pw moves only the probe → withdraw the layer-48 "readers miss the model's errors" claim; replace with "no reader-independent linear method reads a load-bearing off-text answer at this site".
- P1 fails (readers don't follow d_cm) → the class-mean hypothesis is wrong; report the matrix as is and stop.

# PART V: parallel experiments

V1–V4 are CPU-only on cached data and Michael Kenney's released repo (`nla-consistency/` in the repo root; cite him for anything reused). V5 needs a small GPU, not the 27B pod. V6 and V7 are short arms inside W's pod session. Same statistics rules as W.

## V1: Reader layer sweep on the error set (CPU, cached Y1 captures)

**Why.** The claim "a probe reads the model's wrong answer at layer 48 and the readers miss it" is open to "you read the wrong layer". Y1 captured layers 42–62 at the last 12 positions on the 297 puzzle errors and 165 self-choice disagreements.

**Do.** Score J-lens, Oracle Lens (grid layers; nearest grid layer where off-grid) and the transferred NLA at every captured layer, last token, on both sets. Report P(favours the model's actual answer) per reader per layer with item-level bootstrap CI, next to the E10 probe curve and the logit-lens reference.

**Pre-registered outcomes.** (a) A reader crosses 0.75 at some layer ≤ 62 → report the first such layer and the lag behind the probe; claim becomes "present N layers before any reader sees it". (b) No reader crosses 0.75 by layer 62 → the strong claim stands. Report which.

**Output.** `v/v1.md`, `v/v1.csv`, `v/fig_v1.png` (one panel per set, layer on x, reader curves with CIs, probe and logit lens as reference).

## V2: Re-solving test (CPU, cached X3 and Y1 readouts)

**Why.** J-lens at 41% for the model's answer on errors suggests it favours the *correct* answer, i.e. re-solves the puzzle from the vector rather than reading the model's choice.

**Do.** On the 297 errors, per reader and per layer (42 and 48), report P(favours correct) and P(favours model's actual) with CIs, against the correct-answer controls. Add a third candidate set where available (the k−1 "stops one step early" symbol, 151 items) so the three-way preference is visible. Permutation null by shuffling candidate labels within item.

**Pre-registered threshold.** A reader "re-solves" if P(favours correct) − P(favours actual) ≥ 0.15 with CI excluding 0.

**Output.** `v/v2.md`, `v/fig_v2.png` (per reader: stacked preference correct / actual / k−1).

## V3: Sensitivity-based confabulation detector on Michael's released data (CPU)

**Why.** Michael's consensus filter cannot catch claims the NLA repeats consistently but wrongly ("10 − 1" read as "2 − 1" in 8/8 samples). Hypothesis: those claims are generated from the NLA's prior and are invariant to the activation; read claims vary with it.

**Do.** From `nla-consistency/data/` take the NLA samples, claim labels (true/false, input/internal), agreement counts and control vectors. For every state where his data includes readouts on perturbed or control vectors, compute per claim: (i) recurrence across the 8 samples (his score), (ii) a sensitivity score = 1 − P(claim recurs under the random/control vectors of matched norm). If his data lacks per-state perturbed readouts, build the sensitivity score from what exists (random-vector readouts, 29% self-consistency baseline) and state the limitation. Fit a logistic model with (i), (ii) and both; report AUROC for input and internal claims, and specifically on the "repeat in ≥ 7 of 8" wrong internal claims (27 + 12 items).

**Pre-registered threshold.** Sensitivity adds ≥ 0.05 AUROC over recurrence alone on internal claims, CI excluding 0.

**Output.** `v/v3.md`, `v/fig_v3.png`, and a short paragraph for Michael.

## V4: Human labels for claim precision (no compute)

Sample 300 claims stratified across reader (Oracle, NLA), claim type (input, internal) and judge label. Write them to `v/human_labels.csv` with columns claim, prompt, model answer, judge label, blank human label. The operator fills the last column offline. When it returns, compute judge–human κ and re-run W3 precision on the human subset.

## V5: Behaviour-trained window reader (small GPU; not the pod)

**Why.** The "improved tool" prototype. Every current reader is trained on text descriptions of a single vector. Train a small reader on the model's own behaviour over a window of positions, from data already cached.

**Data.** X2/Z4 cached states at layer 38, all positions, for ownership and mapping (natural and edited); Y1 cached states at layers 42–52, last 12 positions, for the error set. Targets: (T1) the model's actual answer (two-way, or 8-way for mapping), (T2) whether an edit flips the answer, (T3) the prompt fact, as a control.

**Models.** (a) last-token linear probe (baseline), (b) mean-pooled linear probe, (c) attention-pooled probe over the window (one learned query, McKenzie-style), (d) a 2-layer MLP on the attention-pooled vector. Capacity-matched where possible. Inner-CV regularisation; hold out pair families; for errors hold out by template.

**Evaluate.** For each target: held-out accuracy with CI; on T2, ΔAUROC over dose-only (as in Z1); on T1 errors, P(favours actual) at layers 42 and 48 next to the readers from V1. Transfer: train on ownership, test on mapping and vice versa.

**Pre-registered success.** Window reader (c or d) beats the best last-token reader on T1 errors by ≥ 10 points with CI excluding 0, or on T2 ΔAUROC by ≥ 0.05. Also report whether it beats the readers on T3 (it should not need to).

**Output.** `v/v5.md`, `v/fig_v5.png`, saved weights under `cache/g_agreement/v/window_reader/`. Keep training under 30 GPU-minutes total.

## V6: Multi-position Oracle Lens (append to W pod session S3, ~15 min)

**Why.** The Oracle takes one vector as a marker-token embedding. The ownership binding at layer 38 is spread over the post-statement span and absent from any single position.

**Do.** On the 64 ownership states at layer 38: feed the Oracle (a) last token only (baseline), (b) the post-statement span as consecutive marker tokens, (c) the full prompt span, (d) the span with positions shuffled (control). Score native log-odds for the ownership question. Also on 32 edited states from W-S2 (full swap, α = 1) to check that span-reading follows the causal edit.

**Pre-registered threshold.** (b) beats (a) by ≥ 10 points within-pair win, CI excluding 0, and (d) is below (b).

**Output.** `v/v6.md`, one table.

## V7: Target-private facts pilot (append to W pod session S1, ~15 min)

**Why.** The one setting where the prompt-only baseline cannot win by construction.

**Do.** 16 ownership pairs where the binding is stated only in a preamble shown to the target model at capture time; the read vector's visible prompt (the text any prompt-only baseline sees) is identical across the pair. Capture at layers 38 and 42, last token and post-statement span. Score all readers natively. Prompt-only baseline on the visible text must be at 50%.

**Pre-registered threshold.** Any reader ≥ 0.70 within-pair win with CI excluding 0.5 is a positive pilot; design a 48-pair confirmatory set.

**Output.** `v/v7.md`, one table, and the 48-pair design if positive.
